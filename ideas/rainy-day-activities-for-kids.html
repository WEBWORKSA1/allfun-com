50 Rainy Day Activities for Kids

Family9 min readUpdated 2026-10-29By the AllFun editors

Rain on the window, kids bouncing off the walls and a whole day ahead of you. It's a familiar scene. The good news is that a soggy day is a perfect excuse to try things you never get around to: a blanket fort big enough for three, a homemade volcano, a living-room obstacle course.

Here are 50 rainy day activities for kids, grouped into crafts, active indoor games, science at home, quiet play and screen-smart options. Most use things you already have. Ages vary, so adapt as you go, and keep an adult nearby for anything involving scissors, the stove or small parts around little ones.

Easy crafts with household supplies

  1. Paper plate masks. Cut eye holes (adults handle the scissors for young kids), then decorate with markers, yarn and scrap paper. Finish with a mask parade.
  2. Toilet roll animals. Turn cardboard tubes into owls, cats or octopuses with paint, paper scraps and googly eyes.
  3. Salt dough ornaments. Mix 1 cup flour, ½ cup salt and about ½ cup water into a dough. Cut out shapes and let them air-dry over a day or two (or have an adult bake them on low heat), then paint.
  4. Rainy window art. Draw pictures on the inside of a window with washable window markers and watch the rain "move" behind them.
  5. Cardboard box city. Collect boxes and build a town with roads drawn in marker. Toy cars move in right away.
  6. Paper chain countdown. Make a chain with one loop for each day until a birthday or holiday. Kids tear one off every morning.
  7. Sock puppets. Glue on buttons (for older kids only), felt and yarn, then write a short puppet show.
  8. Nature collage. Use leaves, twigs and pebbles collected on the last dry day. Glue them onto cardboard to make a picture.
  9. Homemade modeling dough. Combine 1 cup flour, ¼ cup salt, ½ cup water, 1 tablespoon oil and a few drops of food coloring. Knead until smooth and store it in a sealed bag.
  10. Paper airplane workshop. Fold three different designs, then test which flies farthest down the hallway.

Active indoor games to burn energy

  1. Living-room obstacle course. Crawl under chairs, hop between cushions and balance along a line of tape. Time each run and try to beat it. Move breakables first.
  2. Freeze dance. Play music and pause it at random. Anyone who moves after the music stops does five jumping jacks.
  3. Balloon volleyball. String a ribbon between two chairs as a net. The balloon can't touch the floor. Keep popped-balloon pieces away from young children, since they can be a choking hazard.
  4. Indoor scavenger hunt. Write clues that lead from room to room, ending at a small prize or snack.
  5. Animal walks relay. Race across the room as a crab, a bear, a frog and a penguin.
  6. Captain says. Classic, free and endlessly adaptable: follow the leader's commands only when they start with "Captain says." Let kids take turns as Captain.
  7. Hide and seek with a twist. Hide a stuffed toy instead of a person, and give "hotter" or "colder" clues.
  8. Sock basketball. Roll up socks and toss them into a laundry basket from different distances. Award more points for longer shots.
  9. Charades for kids. Act out animals, jobs or favorite story characters. The charades generator can supply prompts when imagination runs dry.
  10. Dance party with a dice roll. Assign each number on a die a dance move, roll a die and do the move it lands on.

Science at home (safe and simple)

All of these use everyday kitchen ingredients. An adult should supervise, and kids should never taste the experiments.

  1. Baking soda volcano. Put 2 tablespoons of baking soda in a cup, add a few drops of dish soap and food coloring, then pour in vinegar. Do it on a tray for easy cleanup.
  2. Rainbow walking water. Line up six cups, fill every other one with colored water and connect them with folded paper towels. Over a few hours, the colors travel and mix.
  3. Sink or float. Gather ten small objects, have kids predict which will float, then test them in a bowl of water. Record the results on a chart.
  4. Homemade rain gauge. Set a clear plastic container outside and mark centimeters on it with a ruler and marker. Check how much rain fell by bedtime.
  5. Dancing raisins. Drop raisins into a glass of clear sparkling water and watch them rise and sink as bubbles attach to them.
  6. Magic milk. Pour milk into a shallow dish, add drops of food coloring and touch the center with a cotton swab dipped in dish soap. The colors swirl.
  7. Ice excavation. Freeze small plastic toys in a container of water overnight. In the morning, kids use warm water and a spoon to free them.
  8. Static electricity tricks. Rub a balloon on a wool sweater and use it to lift bits of tissue paper or bend a thin stream of tap water.
  9. Paper helicopter drop. Cut and fold simple paper helicopters, then drop them from the same height to see which design spins slowest.
  10. Shadow puppets and light. Darken a room, shine a flashlight on the wall and experiment with how distance changes shadow size.

Quiet play and calm-down activities

  1. Blanket fort reading nook. Drape blankets over chairs, add pillows and a flashlight and read stories inside.
  2. Jigsaw puzzle race. Give each child a small puzzle and see who finishes first, or team up on a bigger one.
  3. Story stones. Draw simple pictures (a sun, a dog, a house) on flat stones or paper circles. Kids draw three at random and invent a story that uses them all.
  4. Building brick challenge. Call out a challenge, such as "build a bridge," "build something that rolls" or "build a home for a toy," and set a timer.
  5. Sticker storyboards. Give kids stickers and a blank page divided into four boxes. They create a comic strip story.
  6. Card games. Teach a classic like Go Fish, War or Crazy Eights with a standard deck.
  7. Listening game. Close your eyes for one minute and count every sound you hear: rain, the fridge, a dog barking. Compare lists.
  8. Coloring and drawing prompts. Write silly prompts on slips of paper, such as "a cat driving a bus" or "your dream treehouse," and let kids draw one at random.
  9. Letter to a relative. Write and decorate a card for a grandparent or friend, then mail it on the next dry day.
  10. Fun facts reading. Read aloud from our fun facts about animals and let kids pick their favorite to share at dinner.

Screen-smart options

Screens aren't the enemy on a rainy day. The trick is choosing interactive, short sessions and pairing them with off-screen follow-ups.

  1. Memory game showdown. Play memory match in turns and keep a family scoreboard.
  2. Reaction time challenge. Test reflexes with the reaction time game, then do an off-screen version by dropping and catching a ruler.
  3. Pattern repeat game. Pattern Echo builds memory and focus in short rounds. Follow it with a real-life clapping pattern game.
  4. Mole pop for little ones. Mole Pop is quick, simple and great for younger kids practicing hand-eye coordination.
  5. Typing practice race. Older kids can try an online typing test and track their improvement over the week.
  6. Daily brain teaser. Pick a daily brain teaser or riddle together and talk through the answers.
  7. Virtual museum tour. Many museums offer free online tours. Explore one gallery, then draw a favorite piece from memory.
  8. Guided drawing video. Follow a step-by-step drawing tutorial, pausing to finish each step together.
  9. Kids' quiz time. Take a family-friendly online quiz together, guess each other's answers first and compare results.
  10. Make a home movie. Use a phone to film a two-minute "weather report" or puppet show, then watch the premiere with popcorn.

Tips for a smoother rainy day

  • Alternate loud and quiet. Follow an energetic game with a calm activity to avoid meltdowns.
  • Set up a "rainy day box." Keep craft supplies, a deck of cards and a few surprise activities in one bin for days like this. Our free printable Game Night Kit is an easy addition with ready-to-use games and score sheets.
  • Let kids choose. Write activities on slips of paper and let each child draw one.
  • Use timers. Short, defined sessions keep attention high and make transitions easier.
  • Embrace a little mess. Lay down a sheet or tray for crafts and science, then make cleanup a race.

Frequently asked questions

What can kids do indoors on a rainy day without screens?

Plenty. Build a blanket fort, set up an obstacle course, run a scavenger hunt, do a simple kitchen science experiment or make salt dough crafts. Card games, puzzles and story stones are great for quieter moments.

How do I keep kids active when they're stuck inside?

Clear some floor space and try freeze dance, balloon volleyball, animal walk relays or an obstacle course. Short bursts of movement every hour or so help burn energy and make quiet activities easier afterward.

What are safe science experiments for kids at home?

Stick to everyday kitchen ingredients like baking soda, vinegar, milk, food coloring, dish soap and water. Experiments such as a baking soda volcano, walking water or sink-or-float are simple and safe with adult supervision. Avoid anything involving heat, sharp tools or cleaning chemicals unless an adult handles that part.

How can I entertain toddlers on a rainy day?

Keep activities short and sensory: homemade modeling dough, sink or float, sock basketball and hide-the-toy games work well. Supervise closely, skip small parts and balloons that could be choking hazards, and plan for frequent switches.

How much screen time is okay on a rainy day?

Every family sets its own limits. A helpful approach is to choose interactive games or creative videos over passive watching, keep sessions short and follow each one with an off-screen activity inspired by it.
