35 Party Games for Adults (No Drinking Required)

Party9 min readUpdated 2026-10-29By the AllFun editors

Grown-ups love to play. They just need permission and a game with rules short enough to explain while someone's still hanging up their coat. Every game below is clean, inclusive and built for laughter. Nothing needs to be poured, no one gets embarrassed, and nobody has to be "the fun one."

Each entry lists the number of players, a realistic time estimate, the materials and exactly how to play. Most need nothing more than paper, pens and a timer. Scan the sections, pick three or four that suit your crowd, and you've got a party plan.

Easy starters and icebreakers

Use these in the first half hour while people are still arriving and warming up. For more openers, see our full guide to icebreaker games.

1. Two Truths and a Lie

Players: 4–20 · Time: 15–20 min · Materials: None Each person shares three statements about themselves: two true, one false. The group votes on the lie. Points go to anyone who fools the majority. Encourage oddly specific truths, since those make the best fakes.

2. Who Am I?

Players: 6–30 · Time: 15 min · Materials: Sticky notes, pen Write a famous person or fictional character on a sticky note and press it onto each guest's back. Guests mingle, asking yes-or-no questions until they figure out who they are. It's a natural way to get strangers talking.

3. Human Bingo

Players: 8–40 · Time: 15–20 min · Materials: Bingo cards, pens Each square describes something like "has visited three continents" or "can whistle a tune." Guests hunt for people who match and collect signatures. The first full line wins. Ready-made sheets are in our free printable Game Night Kit.

4. The Hot Seat

Players: 5–12 · Time: 20 min · Materials: Timer One player sits in the hot seat for 60 seconds while the group fires light questions such as "best vacation?" or "worst haircut?" Answers must come instantly. Hesitate for more than three seconds and you owe the group a second round.

5. Baby Photo Lineup

Players: 6–20 · Time: 15 min · Materials: Childhood photos from each guest Collect photos in advance, number them and pin them up. Guests write down who they think each baby is. The most correct matches wins. Plan on lots of "how is that you?!"

6. Would You Rather Showdown

Players: 4–20 · Time: 10–20 min · Materials: Phone or laptop Read a dilemma from our would you rather generator. Guests move to one side of the room or the other, then each side gets 30 seconds to persuade someone to switch. Converts earn their side a point.

Word and talking games

Perfect for a couch full of people and a coffee table full of snacks.

7. Forbidden Words

Players: 4–16, in two teams · Time: 20–30 min · Materials: Homemade cards, timer Each card shows a target word plus four forbidden words you can't say. One player describes; their team guesses as many cards as possible in a minute. Say a forbidden word and the card goes to the other team.

8. Category Race

Players: 3–10 · Time: 20 min · Materials: Paper, pens, timer Pick a random letter and read out ten categories, like "a city," "a dessert" or "something in a garage." Everyone has two minutes to write one answer per category starting with that letter. Unique answers score a point; duplicates score nothing.

9. Fake Definitions

Players: 4–10 · Time: 30 min · Materials: A dictionary, paper slips The reader picks an obscure word and writes down its real definition. Everyone else writes a convincing fake one. The reader shuffles and reads them all aloud, and players vote. You score for picking the truth and for every vote your fake receives.

10. Contact

Players: 4–10 · Time: 20 min · Materials: None The leader thinks of a word and reveals only its first letter. Others give clues for any word starting with those letters. If another player guesses the clue-giver's word and both shout "contact!" before the leader blocks it, the leader reveals another letter.

11. Ghost

Players: 3–8 · Time: 15 min · Materials: None Players take turns adding one letter to a growing word fragment. Whoever completes a real word of four or more letters earns a letter of G-H-O-S-T. Bluffing is allowed, but anyone can challenge you to name the word you're building.

12. One-Word Clues

Players: 4–12, in pairs · Time: 20 min · Materials: Word cards One partner sees a secret word and gives a single-word clue. If their partner misses, the next pair gets a clue and a guess. Points shrink with every clue, so short, sharp hints win.

13. The Outsider

Players: 4–10 · Time: 15 min · Materials: Paper slips Everyone but one player gets a slip with the same location, like "a bakery." The outsider gets a blank slip. Players take turns asking each other questions about the place, then vote on who's bluffing. The outsider wins by naming the location.

14. Random-Word Story Chain

Players: 4–12 · Time: 15 min · Materials: Word slips in a bowl Players build a story one sentence at a time, and each person must draw a random word from the bowl and work it in naturally. Words like "pineapple," "submarine" and "grandmother" lead to plot twists no one sees coming.

Drawing and acting games

These games deliver the biggest laughs per minute.

15. Classic Charades

Players: 6–20, in two teams · Time: 30 min · Materials: Prompts, timer Act out a movie, book or phrase without speaking while your team guesses within one minute. Our charades generator supplies fresh prompts, so no one has to brainstorm.

16. Reverse Charades

Players: 8–20, in teams · Time: 20 min · Materials: Prompts, timer Flip the usual setup: the whole team acts out the prompt together while one teammate guesses. The chaos of five people trying to be "a washing machine" is the whole point.

17. Fishbowl

Players: 6–20, in two teams · Time: 40 min · Materials: Paper slips, a bowl, timer Everyone writes five names or nouns. In round one, describe them freely; round two, use only one word; round three, act them out silently. The same slips return each round, so inside jokes build fast.

18. Drawing Relay

Players: 6–16, in teams · Time: 20 min · Materials: Large paper or whiteboard, markers A host whispers a word to one player from each team. They race to draw it for their teammates, with no letters or numbers allowed. The first team to guess earns a point, and a new artist steps up.

19. Draw-and-Pass Telephone

Players: 5–12 · Time: 20 min · Materials: Small stacks of paper, pens Everyone writes a phrase on the first page and passes it on. The next person draws it, the next writes what they think the drawing shows, and so on. Reveal each stack at the end and watch "a dog on a bike" become "space soup."

20. Back-to-Back Drawing

Players: Any even number · Time: 15 min · Materials: Paper, pens, simple images Partners sit back to back. One describes a simple image without naming it ("draw a circle, add two triangles on top…"), while the other draws. Compare the results and vote on the closest match.

21. Hum That Tune

Players: 4–20 · Time: 15 min · Materials: Song list A player hums a well-known song with no words allowed, and the room races to name it. Use categories like "movie themes" or "songs from the '90s" to keep things fair.

22. Forehead Guess

Players: 4–12 · Time: 15 min · Materials: Index cards A player holds a card to their forehead without looking. Teammates give clues and the player has one minute to guess as many cards as possible. Pass to skip.

Active and silly games

Push the coffee table aside for these.

23. One-Minute Challenges

Players: 4–20 · Time: 30 min · Materials: Cups, cotton balls, straws, cookies Run a series of 60-second tasks: stack 21 cups into a pyramid, move cotton balls between bowls with a spoon held in your mouth, or wiggle a cookie from your forehead to your mouth with no hands. Score each event.

24. Spoons

Players: 4–10 · Time: 15 min · Materials: A deck of cards, spoons (one fewer than players) Pass cards quickly around the circle, trying to collect four of a kind. When someone gets four, they quietly take a spoon, and everyone else grabs for one. Whoever's left empty-handed earns a letter.

25. Bottle Flip Challenge

Players: 2–20 · Time: 10 min · Materials: Plastic bottles about one-third full of water Each player gets five flips and scores a point for every bottle that lands upright. Add rounds for left hand only or landing on a book.

26. Sardines

Players: 6–15 · Time: 20–30 min · Materials: A home with hiding spots One person hides. Everyone else searches, and anyone who finds the hider quietly squeezes in with them. The last seeker hides next round. Agree on off-limits rooms first.

27. Lip-Sync Showdown

Players: 4–12 · Time: 30 min · Materials: Music, a speaker, props Players pick a song and perform a full lip-sync with dramatic gestures and props. The audience scores on commitment, not accuracy. Hats and hairbrush microphones are highly encouraged.

28. Human Knot

Players: 6–12 · Time: 10 min · Materials: None Stand in a circle, reach across and grab two different hands. Without letting go, untangle into a single circle. Gentle movements only, and anyone can call a pause.

Team and competition games

Great for bigger parties where you want a winner at the end of the night.

29. Living-Room Trivia

Players: 6–30, in teams · Time: 45–60 min · Materials: Question sheets, answer pads Write five rounds of six questions on themes like geography, food and music. Teams submit answers after each round. Our team picker builds balanced teams in seconds.

30. Guess the Group's Answer

Players: 6–20, in two teams · Time: 30 min · Materials: Pre-surveyed questions Before the party, ask guests questions like "name a breakfast food." Teams try to guess the most popular answers. Higher-ranked answers earn more points.

31. How Well Do You Know Me?

Players: 4–16, in pairs · Time: 20 min · Materials: Whiteboards or paper Partners answer questions about each other separately, such as "What's their dream job?" or "What would they order at a diner?" Reveal answers together. Matches score a point.

32. Photo Scavenger Hunt

Players: 6–30, in teams · Time: 45–60 min · Materials: Phones, a list of prompts Give teams 20 prompts like "recreate a famous painting" or "a photo with a stranger's dog (with permission)." Creative interpretations earn bonus points at the reveal.

33. Party Mystery

Players: 8–16 · Time: 60–90 min · Materials: Character cards, clue cards Write a light whodunit around a theft, such as the missing birthday cake. Each guest gets a character with a secret and a goal. Clues come out over the evening, and everyone makes a final accusation before the host reveals the culprit.

34. Big-Screen Reflex Duel

Players: 2–16 · Time: 20 min · Materials: A laptop connected to a TV Players take turns on the reaction time test. Best of three counts, and the fastest two play a final. A typing-speed round makes a great tiebreaker.

35. Desert Island Draft

Players: 4–10 · Time: 20 min · Materials: Paper, pens In turns, each player "drafts" one item, song and book for a desert island from a shared list. After three rounds, everyone pitches their survival kit, and the group votes for the most convincing.

Frequently asked questions

What are good party games for adults that don't involve drinking?

Fishbowl, forbidden-words games, charades, category races, trivia and one-minute challenges are all big hits, and none of them needs anything stronger than lemonade. They rely on wit and teamwork, so the energy comes from the game itself.

What party games work for a large group of adults?

Team-based games scale best: trivia, fishbowl, reverse charades, guess the group's answer and photo scavenger hunts. Split into teams of four to six so everyone stays involved.

What are quick party games that need no supplies?

Two truths and a lie, contact, ghost, the hot seat and the human knot need nothing at all. Charades only needs a list of prompts, which you can generate on a phone.

How do I get shy adults to join in?

Start with team games where no one performs alone, like trivia or reverse charades. Offer non-playing roles such as scorekeeper or judge, and never force anyone into a spotlight game.

How many games should I plan for a party?

Plan four to six, then expect to play three or four. Start with an icebreaker, schedule the most energetic game in the middle, and end with something calm and conversational.
