75 Fun Things to Do at Home

Ideas8 min readUpdated 2026-10-29By the AllFun editors

Home can start to feel like a waiting room when you're running the same three activities on repeat: scroll, snack, sigh. The good news is that your house is already stocked with everything you need for a great day. You just need fresh ways to use it.

Below are 75 ideas sorted by mood, from loud and active to slow and cozy. Most cost nothing, many work for any age, and every one comes with enough detail that you can start in the next ten minutes.

Games and puzzles

  1. Run a household tournament. Pick a card game everyone knows, draw a bracket on paper, tape it to the fridge and play it out over a week.
  2. Race the sudoku clock. Each player tackles the same sudoku puzzle and the fastest solver chooses dinner.
  3. Play sardines. It's hide and seek in reverse: one person hides, and everyone who finds them squeezes into the same spot until the last seeker is left alone.
  4. Write a custom crossword. Fill it with inside jokes and family nicknames, then hand it to someone who'll get every clue.
  5. Set up a rhyming treasure hunt. Write five clues that lead room to room, with a small prize hidden at the end.
  6. Keep the balloon up. The balloon can't touch the floor. Every two minutes, add a rule such as "elbows only" or "one hand behind your back."
  7. Play a roll-and-do game. Assign an action to each number, like 1 for ten jumping jacks or 4 for telling a joke, and take turns on our dice roller.
  8. Build tabletop mini golf. Books become walls, a cup on its side becomes the hole, and a pencil works as a putter.
  9. Make a shoebox marble maze. Glue straws or cardboard strips inside a box lid and tilt it to guide a marble from start to finish.
  10. Flip cards in a memory duel. Take turns on memory match and compare scores, or deal a real deck face down and play the classic way.
  11. Play a drawing-guessing game. One person sketches a secret word on paper while their team guesses before a one-minute timer runs out.
  12. Ask "would you rather" at dinner. Pull fresh questions from our would you rather generator and make everyone defend their answer.

Kitchen fun

  1. Set up a pizza bar. Buy or make plain bases, lay out toppings in bowls and let everyone build their own masterpiece.
  2. Bake an easy no-knead loaf. Flour, water, salt and yeast rest overnight, and you bake it the next day. The smell alone is worth it.
  3. Host a cupcake decorating contest. Provide frosting, sprinkles and candies, then award categories like "most colorful" and "most mysterious."
  4. Have breakfast for dinner. Pancakes, eggs and fruit at 6 p.m. feel strangely rebellious.
  5. Invent a signature smoothie. Blend fruit, yogurt and a surprise ingredient, then give it a name worthy of a café menu.
  6. Cook a relative's recipe. Call a grandparent or aunt, ask for a family dish and let them coach you through it.
  7. Freeze fruit-juice ice pops. Pour juice into small cups, add a few berries, insert sticks and freeze overnight.
  8. Make a one-color meal. Try an all-green dinner: spinach pasta, peas, cucumber salad and green grapes for dessert.
  9. Fold dumplings together. Store-bought wrappers make it easy. Try pleats, half-moons and one deliberately silly shape.
  10. Build a themed snack board. Movie night, rainbow or "foods that start with P." Arrange everything on a big cutting board.
  11. Shake ice cream in a bag. Put milk, sugar and vanilla in a small sealed bag, place it inside a bigger bag of ice and salt, and shake for about ten minutes.

Creative and crafty

  1. Paint a mug. Use ceramic-safe markers and follow the instructions on the package to set the design.
  2. Make a family zine. Fold paper into a small booklet and fill it with comics, recipes, interviews and a horoscope page you made up.
  3. Start a good-news jar. Write one nice thing that happens each day on a scrap of paper and read them all at the end of the month.
  4. Knot friendship bracelets. A simple chevron pattern needs only embroidery thread and a little patience.
  5. Patch or upcycle clothing. Sew a fun patch onto a jacket or turn an old T-shirt into a tote bag with no sewing needed.
  6. Stage a shadow puppet show. Cut characters from cardboard, tape them to sticks and perform against a wall with a lamp behind you.
  7. Make a vision board. Cut images and words from old magazines that match your goals for the next year.
  8. Decorate a plain tote or sneakers. Fabric markers and a stencil turn everyday items into one-of-a-kind pieces.
  9. Design a family crest. Split a shield into four sections and draw something that represents each person's personality.
  10. Make a scrapbook page. Use tickets, photos and doodles from a recent outing before they disappear into a drawer.
  11. Write and perform a short play. Three characters, one problem and a happy ending. Performances happen in the living room at 7 p.m. sharp.
  12. Bake salt dough ornaments. Mix flour, salt and water, cut shapes, bake them low and slow, then paint once they've cooled.

Cozy and relaxing

  1. Build a reading nook. Pile pillows into a corner, add a lamp and a basket of books, and declare it a quiet zone.
  2. Have a spa afternoon. Warm towels, a foot soak, cucumber slices and calm music. Robes are optional but encouraged.
  3. Pair a puzzle with an audiobook. Your hands stay busy and your brain gets a story.
  4. Camp in the living room. Pitch a tent or drape a sheet, tell stories by flashlight and sleep on the floor.
  5. Listen to a full album start to finish. No skipping, no multitasking. Talk about your favorite track afterward.
  6. Set up a hot cocoa bar. Offer toppings like cinnamon, crushed cookies and a few marshmallows per cup.
  7. Write a list of 50 things you love. Big things, tiny things, weird things. It's harder than it sounds and very cheerful.
  8. Try a slow morning. No alarm, breakfast in bed and a book until noon.
  9. Watch the rain and make a soundtrack. Pick the perfect background music for the weather outside.

Get moving indoors

  1. Build an obstacle course. Crawl under chairs, hop between pillows and finish with a balancing walk along a line of tape.
  2. Follow a stretching or yoga video. Twenty minutes on the living-room floor is a great reset after hours of sitting.
  3. Play freeze dance. One person controls the music. When it stops, everyone freezes, and anyone who wobbles sits out a round.
  4. Bowl in the hallway. Line up ten empty plastic bottles and roll a soft ball. Keep score on paper.
  5. Hold a hallway long jump. Mark distances with tape and measure everyone's best of three jumps.
  6. Learn a choreographed dance. Pick a dance tutorial, learn it in sections and perform the full routine.
  7. Have a hoop-spinning contest. Who can keep a hoop going longest around the waist, then around one arm?
  8. Do a wall-sit challenge. Slide down a wall until your knees are bent and hold it. Cheering is allowed and needed.
  9. Play the floor is lava. Scatter cushions as islands and cross the room without touching the floor. Clear sharp corners first.

Learn and explore

  1. Take a virtual museum tour. Many museums offer online galleries. Pick one wing and spend 30 minutes exploring it.
  2. Regrow kitchen scraps. Stand the white roots of green onions in a glass of water and watch new shoots appear within days.
  3. Make a density tower. Slowly layer honey, dish soap, water and oil in a tall glass and watch them stack.
  4. Learn three chords on an instrument. With just three chords on a guitar or ukulele, you can play a surprising number of songs.
  5. Label your house in another language. Sticky notes on the door, fridge and lamp turn every room into a vocabulary lesson.
  6. Explore the universe from your couch. Read our fun facts about space and pick one to research further.
  7. Interview a relative. Ask about their childhood, their first job and the best advice they ever got, and record their answers.
  8. Code a simple web page. Write a few lines of HTML about your favorite hobby and open the file in a browser.
  9. Take apart something broken. An old remote or wind-up toy reveals a lot about how everyday things work.

Home projects that feel like fun

  1. Rearrange one room. Moving a couch or bed can make a space feel brand new without buying anything.
  2. Create a gallery wall. Frame kids' drawings, postcards and photos, and lay them out on the floor before hanging.
  3. Sort your bookshelf by color. It's not the most practical system, but it looks fantastic.
  4. Build a bird feeder. Cut windows in a clean milk carton, fill it with seed and hang it outside a window you can watch from.
  5. Make a family calendar station. A wall calendar, a marker and a spot for notes help everyone see the week ahead.
  6. Play "keep, gift, go." Pick one closet and sort every item into three piles. Set a timer to keep the pace brisk.
  7. Plant a windowsill herb garden. Basil, mint and parsley grow well indoors in good light.

Play together

  1. Host a family game night. Our family fun night ideas guide has themes, snacks and game formats.
  2. Put on a talent show. Everyone gets two minutes, and the audience scores with handmade paddles.
  3. Sing karaoke. Pull up lyric videos, use a hairbrush as a microphone and assign a "song of the night."
  4. Play a game over video call. Twenty questions and trivia both work well with faraway relatives.
  5. Open a home movie theater. Make paper tickets, set up a concession stand and dim the lights.
  6. Hold a monthly awards night. Hand out paper trophies for "best laugh," "most helpful" and "boldest snack choice."

Frequently asked questions

What can I do at home when I'm bored and alone?

Try projects with a visible result: a no-knead loaf, a windowsill herb garden, a shoebox marble maze or a one-day sudoku streak. Cozy solo options include a full-album listening session, a reading nook or a list of 50 things you love.

What are fun things to do at home with family?

Game tournaments, pizza bars, living-room camping, talent shows and treasure hunts all work across ages. Rotate who picks the activity each week so everyone gets a turn to lead.

How can I have fun at home for free?

Most ideas here cost nothing: sardines, freeze dance, hallway bowling with recycled bottles, shadow puppets and kitchen-scrap gardening. Use what you already own and turn it into a challenge with a timer or a score.

What are fun indoor activities for a rainy day?

Obstacle courses, the floor is lava, blanket forts, density towers and cupcake contests all burn energy or spark curiosity without anyone stepping outside.

How do I make staying home feel less boring?

Give your day a shape. Pick one active idea, one creative idea and one cozy idea, and schedule them like real plans. Changing the room you're in or rearranging furniture also helps a familiar space feel new.
